Philosophy

What I think.

I owe myself a good life. Everyone else should get the chance to build one too.

My life is mine. I decide how to live it, and the only limit I accept is that my choices shouldn’t take that same freedom from anyone else.

I don’t need meaning handed to me by a religion or by anyone’s approval. I find it in whatever I’ve decided matters to me. In finishing what I said I’d finish. In making something because I wanted it to exist, and in being some use to people while I’m here.

Most people are decent. When I can make someone’s life easier I usually will. Nobody is owed it — I do it because I want to be that kind of person. But I’m not required to wreck myself to prove the point.

I forgive people when I decide to. Forgiveness isn’t trust. Trust gets rebuilt by what someone does next, if it gets rebuilt at all.

I get things wrong. I try not to beat myself up over decisions made without information I didn’t have yet, and I try not to pretend the damage was nothing. Some regrets are worth keeping. They make me better.

We owe each other something too. No one should go hungry, lose their home, miss out on school, or be ruined by getting sick. People who can contribute should. People who genuinely can’t should still live with dignity. The more you have, the more you can give, and the more you should.

I don’t know if there’s a God, or whether anything comes after this. I’m fine not knowing. I don’t need a reward waiting at the end to be decent now. If someone is keeping score, I hope they were paying attention. 😄

A good life doesn’t have to be an easy one. There will be struggle and failure and loss. What matters is that it was mine — that I chased what I cared about, didn’t stand in anyone else’s way, and left a few people better off for having known me.

I hope I’m remembered kindly.

Work

Two platform generations at Openbridge.

I joined pre-product in 2013 and built the first version of the SaaS application on my own — a Symfony API behind an Angular front end, nothing to production. Architecture, implementation, the third-party integrations, and the Linux and AWS underneath it.

In 2020 we replaced it. Different stack, same domain: I was the primary front-end engineer on the new application and carried the business logic across, walking the developer writing the Python API through the rules and the reasoning already baked into the old system. Two builds, two stacks, one product.

The front end I stood up on Angular 8 runs on Angular 21 today, upgraded the whole way rather than left to rot. Currently building the CLI that lets customers point their AI agents at the API.

Tools

Things I built for myself.

Circle of Fifths

Pick a key, hear its seven chords, see how each one functions. The wheel isn’t decoration — your I, IV and V sit next to each other on it, which is why three chords carry most songs.

String Tuner

Tap a string, hear its pitch, tune to it. No microphone and nothing to install, so it still works in a loud room — which is where pitch detection tends to give up.

Bread Helper

Baker’s percentages, pizza dough ball weights and pan volumes. Set the flour weight or the dough weight and it solves the rest, so a recipe scales to whatever tin you actually own.
